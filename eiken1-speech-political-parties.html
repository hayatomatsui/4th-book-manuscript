Political parties no longer
represent ordinary citizens.

政党はもはや標準的な国民を適切に代表していない — 賛成か反対かを述べる

MY POSITIONAGREE

論の骨格

The skeleton — 日本語で流れを固めてから英語に乗せる

  1. 01 立場賛成。理由は2つ — 政党の内部分裂と、国民側の多様化。
  2. 02 理由① 政党が内部で割れている同じ党の中でも指導者の考えが正反対。だから「特定の政治家は支持するが、その党は支持できない」が起こる。党が一つの立場を代表できないなら、有権者も代表できない。
  3. 03 理由② 国民の側が多様化したかつては正規分布の中央（ボリュームゾーン）を買う人が多く、大量生産が機能した。今は需要が分布の端＝ロングテールへ移った。政党は中央を取りに行く設計のまま、痩せた中央に向けてマス商品を売り続けている。
  4. 04 結論政党が変わったのではない。「標準的な国民」という一つの塊がもう存在しない。

Version A — 推奨

Higher register / 本番で狙う版

Version A ABOUT 255 WORDS  /  ABOUT 1 MIN 55 SEC

I agree with this statement, for two reasons: political parties are internally divided, and the public itself has become far too diverse to be represented by them.

First, parties are no longer unified. Look at Japan today. Prime Minister Takaichi and her predecessor, former Prime Minister Ishiba, both come from the Liberal Democratic Party, the governing party — and yet their views on the economy, security, and social issues are almost opposite. Personally, I support Prime Minister Takaichi, but I cannot honestly say that I support the LDP itself. If a single party cannot represent one consistent position, it cannot represent voters either.

Second, and more fundamentally, the public has become extremely diverse. I once learned in a marketing class that small companies now have an advantage over large ones. In the past, most consumers bought products in the so-called volume zone, the middle of the distribution, so mass production worked well. Today, demand has moved toward the tails of that distribution — to what is often called the long tail. People want products that fit their own particular needs.

Exactly the same thing has happened in politics. Parties are designed to capture the middle of public opinion, but that middle has become thin. They are still trying to sell a mass-market product to a long-tail society.

For these reasons, political parties no longer represent ordinary citizens. It is not that parties have changed, but that "ordinary citizens" no longer exist as a single group.

黄色の一文が決め台詞。ここで一拍置くと効く。

Version B — 話しやすい

Plainer English / 口が慣れるまではこちらから

Version B ABOUT 180 WORDS  /  ABOUT 1 MIN 20 SEC

I agree with this statement. I have two reasons.

First, parties are divided inside. In Japan, Prime Minister Takaichi and the prime minister before her, Mr. Ishiba, are both members of the same party, the Liberal Democratic Party. But their opinions are very different. I support Prime Minister Takaichi, but I cannot say that I support her party. If a party does not have one clear opinion, it cannot represent people's opinions.

Second, people themselves have become very different from each other. When I was a graduate student, a marketing teacher said that small companies will become stronger. In the past, most people bought the same popular products, so big companies were strong. But now people want many different things, not only the most popular ones. This is called the long tail.

Politics is the same. Parties try to represent the biggest group of people, but that group is getting smaller and smaller. So it is difficult for parties to represent everyone.

For these reasons, I think political parties no longer represent ordinary people.

2分に対して40秒ほど余る。焦らず、ゆっくり話す余裕として使う。
